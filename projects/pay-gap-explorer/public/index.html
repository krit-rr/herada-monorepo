    Reading it

    What a gap here does and doesn’t mean

    The unit. Medicare values every procedure in relative value units: a work RVU for the clinician’s time, skill and stress, plus practice-expense and malpractice RVUs. The total, times one national conversion factor, is the payment before geographic adjustment. The work RVU is the part that says how hard Medicare thinks the operation is, which is why the literature compares it first.

    Global periods. A code with a 90-day global includes the operation plus three months of routine follow-up; a 10-day or 0-day code does not. Pairs whose global periods differ are flagged, because their RVUs are not the same kind of number.

    Who sets the values. Work RVUs come from physician surveys run through the AMA’s Relative Value Scale Update Committee, which CMS usually accepts. The pairs here were valued by different specialty societies at different times, and that history is part of what the gaps show.

    Codes, not paychecks. Medicare’s conversion factor is the same for a urologist and a gynecologist. Polan and Barber found the remaining dollar gap in how employers pay per RVU, which differs by specialty. This page compares what the codes are worth, not what any surgeon takes home.

    Pairs are judgment calls. Anatomy is matched; the operations are never identical. The published pairs carry their authors’ judgment, the proposed pairs carry Herada’s until a clinician has checked them, and the same-operation pairs are the only ones where the code itself does the matching.

    Why it reaches beyond Medicare. Most private insurers price from the same RVUs, so a code valued low is valued low almost everywhere.
