        Who gets studied

        The trial gap: patients vs. participants

        For ten conditions whose patients are mostly women: the share of women among clinical-trial participants, against their share among patients, from ClinicalTrials.gov posted results. Below the diagonal, women are underrepresented relative to the patient population.

        Scroll sideways for the full chart.

        Reading it: each circle is a condition, sized by participants counted. On the diagonal, trials enroll women at the rate the condition affects them. Both axes start at 50%. Hover any circle for the numbers.

        Caveats: results are posted for only a fraction of registered trials (the last column shows how many per condition), and the patient shares are approximations of commonly cited sex ratios, each from the review linked in the table. Single-sex trials are included. Trials reporting sex in a custom format are counted as unparsed.

          What you’ve heard that doesn’t hold up

          Figures that circulate widely but trace to no primary source, plus anything graded “secondary.” None of these appear as data points anywhere on this page. They are listed so you know we looked + what to cite instead.

            How to read the grades

              Registry and claims figures count whole populations and are the strongest; cohorts count one clinic's patients; surveys count the people who answered, who are usually the people with the worst journeys. Where a widely quoted number failed verification, the traceable number is shown instead.
