Who gets studied?

Some conditions affect mostly women. This page asks a simple question of the public trial registry: for those conditions, what share of clinical-trial participants are women — and does it match the share of patients who are?

Reading it: each circle is a condition, sized by participants counted. The diagonal is parity: on the line, trials enroll women at the rate the condition affects them. Below the line, women are underrepresented relative to the patient population; above it, overrepresented. Both axes start at 50%.

Method, and what to watch for

The denominator problem: results are posted for only a fraction of registered trials (the table shows exactly how many per condition). This chart can only read what sponsors posted, and trials that post results may differ from trials that don't.

What was counted: interventional studies on ClinicalTrials.gov with posted results whose baseline table reports the standard "Sex: Female, Male" measure. Trials reporting sex in a custom format are counted as unparsed. Single-sex trials (for example, trials recruiting only women) are included, since they are part of how a condition gets studied.

The prevalence shares are approximations of commonly cited sex ratios, each from the published review linked in the table. Reasonable sources differ by a few points; the chart's story holds across those ranges.

Enrollment data: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (public domain). Prevalence sex ratios: per-condition sources in the table above. Analysis by Herada; the pull script and per-trial counts ship in the project repository.
