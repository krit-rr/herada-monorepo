The Misrouting Registry

A consented, research-only survey of how midlife women get routed between specialties before the right diagnosis. Results are published and never sold.

What it is

Herada's thesis is that women in their forties and fifties with fatigue, brain fog, joint pain, palpitations or mood change are handed a perimenopause or anxiety explanation at the first visit, before thyroid, iron, autoimmune, cardiac or ADHD causes are considered, or the reverse. The published evidence for that detour is thin and scattered, which is why the Diagnostic Detour exists. The Registry is how we add to it: a structured account of real journeys, collected with consent, analysed with an academic partner and published in full.

What's asked

The visits, in order: who you saw, what they said, what was tested, what was tried. The time from first symptom to the diagnosis that held. Which explanation came first, and what finally changed it. Age band, state, insurance type. Nothing that names you, your clinicians or your clinic.

The verification filter

Only journeys that end in a clinician-confirmed diagnosis are counted in the published analysis. Everyone can submit; the filter keeps self-diagnosis out of the numbers, so the Registry measures the detour to a diagnosis, not the detour to a belief.

Consent, in plain words

  • You choose what to share, and you can stop at any point.
  • Your answers are used for research and publication only, in aggregate, with no way back to you.
  • The survey runs on a separate, consented form service, not on this site, and the terms you agree to are shown there before the first question.
  • You can ask for your entry to be removed before publication.

What happens to the results

They are published: on this site, and with the academic partner, named here before the survey opens. They are never sold, licensed or shared with advertisers, insurers or employers. The data model is the same one the Ledger uses, so what a woman keeps for her own visits and what the Registry measures line up.
