Life stages

Each stage opens with what’s covered: the screening and care a plan already has to pay for, and where that rule comes from. The text of each stage follows as it is written. The lines below describe private plans that follow the Affordable Care Act’s preventive rule (in network, for the screening itself), Medicaid within each state’s rules, or Medicare Part B, as marked. That rule stood up in court in June 2025 (Kennedy v. Braidwood), and the services on it are still covered as of November 2026. Screening is free; follow-up of an abnormal result often isn’t.

Teens and early twenties

What’s covered

  • A yearly preventive visit is covered from adolescence on, with no copay in network. Source: HRSA Women’s Preventive Services Guidelines (WPSI); healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • The HPV vaccine is covered through age 26. The number of doses is being revised in 2026, so ask what the current schedule is. Source: CDC, HPV vaccination; healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • If you are sexually active and 24 or younger, chlamydia and gonorrhea screening is covered, with no copay for the test itself. Source: USPSTF, 2021 (grade B). Checked November 2026.
  • Birth control: plans must cover at least one option in every FDA-approved method, with counseling, at no copay when prescribed. On Medicaid, family planning carries no copays. Source: healthcare.gov; medicaid.gov. Checked November 2026.

Twenties and thirties

What’s covered

  • Cervical cancer screening is covered from 21 to 65 with no copay in network: a Pap test every three years, and from 30 an HPV test every five years, or both together. Source: USPSTF, 2018 (grade A); healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • From 30 to 65 you can ask whether your clinic offers a self-collected HPV sample. The body that writes the HRSA guidelines says it should be offered as an option. Source: WPSI, cervical cancer screening (updated 2026). Checked November 2026.
  • At least one well-woman visit a year is covered, and it can be spread over several visits. Pre-pregnancy, prenatal, postpartum and between-pregnancy visits count. Source: WPSI, well-woman preventive visits. Checked November 2026.
  • Follow-up of an abnormal result (a repeat test, a colposcopy, a biopsy) is diagnostic and can be billed. The no-copay rule is for the screening itself. Source: healthcare.gov (“coverage may vary”). Checked November 2026.

Pregnancy and the year after

What’s covered

  • Every Marketplace and Medicaid plan covers pregnancy and childbirth, even if the pregnancy began before the coverage did. Labor, delivery, hospital care and most tests follow your plan’s usual deductible and coinsurance. Source: healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • Prenatal and postpartum visits are part of the covered well-woman visit. On Medicaid, pregnancy-related care carries no copays. Plans are measured on a first-trimester visit and a postpartum visit between one and twelve weeks after delivery. Source: WPSI; medicaid.gov; NCQA, PPC. Checked November 2026.
  • Depression screening during pregnancy and after birth is covered with no copay, including at your baby’s well visits. Plans are measured on follow-up within 30 days of a positive screen. Source: USPSTF, 2023 (grade B); healthcare.gov; NCQA, PDS-E. Checked November 2026.
  • If you were on Medicaid or CHIP when the pregnancy ended, coverage now continues for 12 months after, in every state but Arkansas, and in DC. Source: medicaid.gov; state count from Georgetown CCF, February 2026, with Wisconsin in force from June 2026. Checked November 2026.

Midlife: forties and fifties

What’s covered

  • A screening mammogram is covered with no copay from age 40, every one to two years. Extra images or a biopsy after it are often billed as diagnostic, so ask how they will be billed. Source: USPSTF, 2024 (grade B); WPSI; healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • Menopause hormone therapy is a covered outpatient drug on Medicaid in every state when its maker takes part in the federal rebate program, within the state’s preferred drug list and prior-authorization rules. A 2026 study found women on Medicaid used it at half the odds of privately insured women (10.7% vs 20.7%). Source: medicaid.gov, Medicaid Drug Rebate Program; Chesnokova et al., JAMA Network Open, July 2026. Checked November 2026.
  • Counseling to prevent weight gain in midlife is covered for women 40 to 60 with a BMI of 18.5 to 29.9, under the HRSA guideline added in 2021. Source: WPSI, preventing obesity in midlife women; Department of Labor FAQ Part 54. Checked November 2026.
  • Bone density screening is covered before 65 for postmenopausal women at increased fracture risk, as judged by a clinician, and for all women 65 and over. Cervical screening continues to 65 on the same terms as above. Source: USPSTF, 2025 (grade B); healthcare.gov. Checked November 2026.

Sixty-five and after

What’s covered

  • Medicare Part B covers a screening mammogram every 12 months with no upper age limit, at no cost if the provider accepts assignment. A diagnostic mammogram costs 20% after the deductible. Source: medicare.gov. Checked November 2026.
  • Medicare covers a bone density test every 24 months, at no cost with assignment, when a condition is documented, such as estrogen deficiency with osteoporosis risk or an X-ray showing possible osteoporosis. After a fracture at 67 to 85, Medicare plans are measured on offering a test or a medicine within six months. Source: medicare.gov; NCQA, OMW. Checked November 2026.
  • Routine cervical screening can stop after 65 with adequate prior screening: three negative Pap tests or two negative co-tests in the last ten years, the latest within five. Whether yours counts is a clinical judgment, so ask. Medicare still covers a Pap test and pelvic exam every 24 months at no cost with assignment. Source: USPSTF, 2018; medicare.gov. Checked November 2026.
